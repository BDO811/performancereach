# Review reply guide (Google Business Profile)

> Read by: reviews-reputation, which drafts replies with Claude. Use the same guide for reviews that arrive through Podium or NiceJob.
> One block per kind of review. Who approves each kind lives in review-flow.md.
> Every reply passes brand-brain/tone-rules.md. A reply never argues, never shares a customer's details and never promises what messaging-bank.md does not back.

## Every reply
- Opens with: [FILL IN: e.g. the reviewer's first name as shown on the review]
- Signed by: [FILL IN: a first name, a role, or the brand]
- Length: [FILL IN]
- Never include: order numbers, phone numbers, email addresses, anything private about the reviewer
- Never ask for a changed rating

## 5 stars
- Goal: thank them for something specific they said
- Shape: thanks + the detail they mentioned + an invitation back
- Must include: [FILL IN]

Example (Northwind Coffee): "Thanks, Sam. Glad the Ethiopia roast made your Sunday. See you for the next bag."

## 4 stars
- Goal: thank them and pick up the one thing that held back the fifth star
- Shape: thanks + what they liked + a short answer to the gap
- Must include: [FILL IN]

## 3 stars
- Goal: show you read it, fix what you can, take detail offline
- Shape: thanks + the issue in their words + what you are doing + how to reach you
- Contact line: [FILL IN: the public contact point you are happy to share]

## 1 or 2 stars
The playbook one-star-review-lands reads this. These replies always wait for the owner.
- Goal: calm, sorry for the experience, move it to a private conversation
- Shape: sorry + the issue in their words, without admitting fault the facts do not support + the contact line + who will help
- Must include: [FILL IN]
- Never: blame the customer, guess what happened, offer a refund or discount without the owner's OK

Example (Northwind Coffee): "Sorry your order arrived late, Alex. That is not the experience we want for you. Please reach us at [contact] so we can put it right."
